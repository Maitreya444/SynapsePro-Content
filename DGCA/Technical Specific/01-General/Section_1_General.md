# Engine Maximum Power

Question

What is the maximum rated horsepower and engine speed (RPM) of the Cessna 172R engine?

Answer

The Cessna 172R has a rated maximum power of **160 Brake Horsepower (BHP)** at an engine speed of **2400 RPM**.

Explanation

The Cessna 172R is powered by a Textron Lycoming IO-360-L2A engine, which is a fuel-injected, horizontally opposed, air-cooled, four-cylinder engine. Knowing the maximum rated horsepower and RPM is essential for monitoring engine performance during takeoff, climb, and other high-power operations. Pilots must ensure the engine operates within these limits to avoid excessive wear or damage. This is one of the most commonly tested aircraft specifications in DGCA Technical Specific examinations.

# Propeller Diameter

Question

What is the standard propeller diameter for the McCauley propeller installed on the Cessna 172R?

Answer

The standard propeller diameter is **75 inches**.

Explanation

The Cessna 172R uses a two-bladed, fixed-pitch McCauley propeller (Model 1C235/LFA7570). The propeller diameter directly affects thrust generation, aerodynamic efficiency, and ground clearance. During the preflight inspection, pilots should visually inspect the propeller for damage and ensure adequate ground clearance. DGCA examinations frequently test the standard propeller diameter as a direct factual question.

# Usable Fuel Capacity

Question

What is the total usable fuel capacity of the Cessna 172R fuel system?

Answer

The total usable fuel capacity is **53.0 U.S. gallons**, which is **26.5 gallons in each wing tank**.

Explanation

The aircraft has a total fuel capacity of **56.0 U.S. gallons**, of which **3.0 U.S. gallons** are unusable (1.5 gallons per tank). Only the usable fuel may be considered for flight planning, endurance, and range calculations. Using total fuel instead of usable fuel may result in inaccurate performance planning and reduced fuel reserves. DGCA examinations commonly test the difference between usable and unusable fuel.

# Oil Capacity

Question

What is the oil capacity of the Cessna 172R engine sump?

Answer

The engine sump oil capacity is **8 U.S. Quarts**.

Explanation

The total oil system capacity is **9 U.S. Quarts**, with the engine sump holding **8 U.S. Quarts**. Engine oil lubricates moving parts, assists in cooling, and helps remove contaminants from the engine. During the preflight inspection, pilots should check the oil level using the dipstick. The POH also recommends operating with at least **5 quarts** of oil, while **8 quarts** is recommended for extended flights. DGCA examinations commonly test both sump capacity and total oil capacity.

# Normal Category Maximum Weights

Question

What are the maximum ramp, takeoff, and landing weights for the Cessna 172R in the Normal Category?

Answer

Maximum Ramp Weight: **2457 lbs**

Maximum Takeoff Weight: **2450 lbs**

Maximum Landing Weight: **2450 lbs**

Explanation

The Normal Category limits define the maximum certified operating weights for standard flight operations. The ramp weight is slightly higher than the takeoff weight because it includes the fuel expected to be consumed during engine start, taxi, and run-up before takeoff. Operating above these limits reduces aircraft performance and may exceed structural design limitations. These values are among the most frequently tested figures in DGCA Technical Specific examinations.

# Baggage Area Limits

Question

What are the individual weight limits for Baggage Area 1 and Baggage Area 2, and what is their maximum combined limit in the Normal Category?

Answer

Baggage Area 1: **120 lbs**

Baggage Area 2: **50 lbs**

Maximum Combined Weight: **120 lbs**

Explanation

The baggage compartment has two loading areas with separate structural limits. Although Area 2 permits up to 50 lbs, the total combined baggage carried in both compartments must never exceed **120 lbs**. Exceeding these limits may shift the aircraft's centre of gravity beyond approved limits, reducing stability and controllability. Proper baggage loading is an essential part of every weight and balance calculation.

# Standard Temperature

Question

How is "Standard Temperature" defined at sea level, and what is its standard lapse rate with altitude?

Answer

Standard Temperature is **15°C at sea level pressure altitude**, and it decreases by **2°C for every 1000 feet** of altitude.

Explanation

Standard Temperature is the baseline atmospheric temperature used for aircraft performance calculations. As altitude increases, temperature decreases at a standard rate of 2°C per 1000 feet. This standard atmosphere is used in performance charts, climb calculations, takeoff distance calculations, and density altitude computations. Understanding this concept is fundamental for interpreting the aircraft's performance data published in the POH.

# Pressure Altitude

Question

What is "Pressure Altitude" as defined in the Pilot's Operating Handbook?

Answer

Pressure Altitude is the altitude indicated when the altimeter is set to **29.92 inches of mercury (1013 hPa)**.

Explanation

Pressure Altitude is the starting point for aircraft performance calculations. By setting the altimeter to the standard pressure setting of 29.92 inHg (1013 hPa), pilots obtain pressure altitude, which is then corrected for non-standard temperature to determine density altitude. Pressure altitude is used throughout the POH performance charts and is one of the most important aviation altitude references.

# Static RPM

Question

What is Static RPM, and what is the normal static RPM range at full throttle for the Cessna 172R?

Answer

Static RPM is the engine RPM achieved at full throttle while the aircraft is stationary on the ground.

The normal range is **2065 to 2165 RPM**.

Explanation

Static RPM is checked to verify that the engine is capable of producing sufficient power before takeoff. Since the aircraft uses a fixed-pitch propeller, the engine cannot reach its maximum rated RPM while stationary. A static RPM outside the specified range may indicate engine or propeller problems requiring maintenance before flight.

# Reference Datum

Question

What is the Reference Datum for weight and balance purposes, and where is it located on the Cessna 172R?

Answer

The Reference Datum is an imaginary vertical plane from which all horizontal distances are measured.

On the Cessna 172R it is located at the **lower portion of the front face of the firewall**.

Explanation

The Reference Datum serves as the zero point for all weight and balance calculations. Every aircraft component has an arm measured from this datum, allowing pilots to calculate moments and determine the aircraft's centre of gravity. Accurate weight and balance calculations ensure the aircraft remains within approved stability and controllability limits.
