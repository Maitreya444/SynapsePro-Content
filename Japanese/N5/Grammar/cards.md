# G001 - N desu

Question
What does 「Nです」mean?

Answer
Polite copula: states "I am / this is [noun]" — identifies or describes something with a noun.

Marathi
मी विद्यार्थी आहे. — I am a student. (Marathi's sentence-final copula "आहे" mirrors Japanese's
sentence-final "です" — both languages put the "to be" verb at the very end.)

Example
Easy: わたしは学生です。 — I am a student. (Why: the plain N+です pattern with no modifiers, the simplest form.)
Medium: わたしは日本語(にほんご)の学生です。 — I am a student of Japanese. (Why: adds a modifying phrase before the noun via の, showing です still just attaches to the final noun.)
Hard: わたしは今年(ことし)から日本語学校(にほんごがっこう)の学生です。 — Since this year, I am a student at a Japanese language school. (Why: embeds a time phrase and a longer noun phrase before です, testing recognition of the pattern under heavier modification.)

Mnemonic
です sounds like a soft stamp — "desu!" — placed at the very end of a sentence to seal the
identification, like a rubber stamp confirming "yes, this is [noun]."

AI Notes
Common mistake: beginners try to conjugate です like a regular verb, or drop it after い-adjectives
thinking it's not needed (大きいです is correct — です still appears for politeness even though the
adjective itself carries the negation/tense later, e.g. 大きくないです). Remember です itself carries
no meaning beyond politeness — the identification comes purely from word order (X は Y です).

# G002 - N dewa arimasen

Question
What does 「Nではありません」mean?

Answer
Polite negative copula: states "I am not / this is not [noun]".

Marathi
मी शिक्षक नाही. — I am not a teacher. (Marathi negates with "नाही" after the noun, paralleling how
Japanese negates with "ではありません" after N — both put the negation marker at the very end.)

Example
Easy: わたしは先生ではありません。 — I am not a teacher. (Why: the basic negative copula attached directly to a noun.)
Medium: これは新(あたら)しい本ではありません。 — This is not a new book. (Why: shows ではありません after a noun modified by an adjective.)
Hard: 田中(たなか)さんは今日(きょう)、学校(がっこう)の先生ではありません。 — Mr. Tanaka is not a school teacher today. (Why: combines a time expression and a modified noun phrase before the negative copula.)

Mnemonic
Break it down: では (as for that) + ありません (there isn't/doesn't exist) — literally "as for that,
it doesn't exist [as that]." では is です's negative doorway; ありません slams it shut.

AI Notes
Common mistake: writing じゃありません and ではありません as if they're different in meaning — they
aren't; じゃ is just the casual-but-still-polite contraction of では, both equally correct in polite
speech. Also watch word order: the negation attaches to the whole です, not to the noun itself, so
never insert ない directly onto the noun.
