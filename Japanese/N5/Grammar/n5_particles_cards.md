# 1. は (Topic marker)

Question
What does 「は」mean?

Answer
Topic marker: indicates what the sentence is about (pronounced "wa").

Marathi
मी विद्यार्थी आहे (mī vidyārthī āhe) — I am a student. (Note the SOV structural parallel: the topic prefix is followed by the noun complement and ends with the verb copula, matching the Japanese pattern).

Example
Easy: 私は学生です。(わたしはがくせいです) — I am a student. (Why: The particle marks the main topic of the sentence.)
Medium: 今日は天気がいいですね。(きょうはてんきがいいですね) — The weather is nice today, isn't it? (Why: The particle highlights today as the temporal topic.)
Hard: あの人は英語が上手ではありません。(あのひとはえいごがじょうずではありません) — That person is not good at English. (Why: The particle marks the person as the topic in a compound negative description.)

Mnemonic
は (pronounced "wa") is like waving a flag: "as for THIS topic..." — setting the stage for what
comes next.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing は (topic — what we're talking ABOUT) with が (subject — WHO/WHAT
specifically does it). は sets the broad stage; が pinpoints the actor. Both can appear in the same
sentence doing different jobs.

# 2. が (Subject marker)

Question
What does 「が」mean?

Answer
Subject marker: indicates the specific subject that performs an action or exists.

Marathi
तिथे एक मांजर आहे — There is a cat there. (The subject of existence is expressed structurally at the start of the clause, mirroring the target of the Japanese particle).

Example
Easy: 犬がいます。(いぬがいます) — There is a dog. (Why: The particle marks the specific animate subject that exists.)
Medium: このりんごが美味しいです。(このりんごがおいしいです) — This apple is delicious. (Why: The particle specifies this particular apple as the subject carrying the adjective.)
Hard: 私にはお姉さんが三人います。(わたしにはおねえさんがさんにんいます) — As for me, I have three older sisters. (Why: The particle marks the subject of possession/existence in a multi-clause sentence.)

Mnemonic
が sounds sharp, like "gah!" — a pointing finger singling out exactly WHO or WHAT.

AI Notes
Common mistake: overusing が when は would sound more natural — が highlights specific or new
information (e.g. answering "who?"), while は introduces an already-known topic. Reaching for が
everywhere makes speech sound oddly emphatic.

# 3. を (Object marker)

Question
What does 「を」mean?

Answer
Object marker: indicates the direct object of a transitive verb.

Marathi
मी आंबा खातो — I eat a mango. (The direct object "आंबा" immediately precedes the verb "खातो", perfectly mirroring the Japanese SOV structure).

Example
Easy: 水を飲みます。(みずをのみます) — I drink water. (Why: The particle marks the direct object of the verb.)
Medium: 毎日日本語を勉強します。(まいにちにほんごをべんきょうします) — I study Japanese every day. (Why: The particle marks the noun as the direct object of study.)
Hard: 教室で面白い本を読んでいます。(きょうしつでおもしろいほんをよんでいます) — I am reading an interesting book in the classroom. (Why: The particle marks the modified noun phrase as the object of the continuous verb.)

Mnemonic
を looks like a little basket carrying the object right up to the verb.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing を (the object marker, also pronounced "o") with お (the honorific prefix,
also "o") — they sound identical but never do the same job; を only ever marks a direct object.

# 4. に (time) (at/on specific time)

Question
What does 「に (time)」mean?

Answer
At/on: marks a specific, numerical point in time when an action occurs.

Marathi
मी सात वाजता उठतो — I wake up at 7 o'clock. (The time suffix "ता" (at) functions as a postposition, directly mirroring the Japanese temporal marker).

Example
Easy: 七時に起きます。(しちじにおきます) — I wake up at 7 o'clock. (Why: The particle marks the specific numerical hour.)
Medium: 朝は六時半に家を出ます。(あさはろくじはんにいえをでます) — In the morning, I leave home at 6:30. (Why: The particle marks the exact temporal point of departure.)
Hard: 来週の日曜日に友達と海へ行きます。(らいしゅうのにちようびにともだちとうみへいきます) — Next week on Sunday, I will go to the sea with my friend. (Why: The particle marks the specific calendar day in a complex motion sentence.)

Mnemonic
に pinpoints an exact dot on the clock — "at THIS precise moment."

AI Notes
Common mistake: adding に after relative time words like 今日/明日/毎日 (today, tomorrow, every day) —
those never take に (❌ 今日に行きます). Only specific/numbered times and days do (七時に, 月曜日に).

# 5. に (direction/target) (to, toward)

Question
What does 「に (direction/target)」mean?

Answer
To, toward, or at: indicates the destination of movement or the target/recipient of an action.

Marathi
मी शाळेत जातो — I go to school. (The directional suffix "त" functions as a postposition, directly parallel to the Japanese movement marker).

Example
Easy: 学校に行きます。(がっこうにいきます) — I go to school. (Why: The particle marks the target destination of the movement.)
Medium: 友達にプレゼントをあげました。(ともだちにプレゼントをあげました) — I gave a present to my friend. (Why: The particle marks the recipient of the active transaction.)
Hard: 部屋に入って、椅子に座ってください。(へやにはいって、いすにすわってください) — Please enter the room and sit on the chair. (Why: The particles mark the destination of entry and the physical target of contact respectively.)

Mnemonic
に points like an arrow straight at a destination or at the person receiving something.

AI Notes
Common mistake: mixing up this に (arrival point / target) with で (the place where an action itself
happens) — に is about WHERE you're headed or WHO receives something, not where the activity takes
place.

# 6. で (location of action) (at/in doing something)

Question
What does 「で (location of action)」mean?

Answer
At, in: indicates the physical location where an active event or action takes place.

Marathi
मी लायब्ररीमध्ये अभ्यास करतो — I study in the library. (The postposition "मध्ये" denotes the location of the action, showing structural parity with the Japanese event marker).

Example
Easy: 図書館で勉強します。(としょかんでべんきょうします) — I study at the library. (Why: The particle marks the physical place where the active verb occurs.)
Medium: レストランで友達と晩ご飯を食べました。(レストランでともだちとばんごはんをたべました) — I ate dinner with my friend at a restaurant. (Why: The particle marks the restaurant as the active setting of the meal.)
Hard: 昨日は自分の部屋で日本語の手紙を書きました。(きのうはじぶんのへやでにほんごのてがみをかきました) — Yesterday, I wrote a Japanese letter in my room. (Why: The particle marks the private space as the location where the writing action occurred.)

Mnemonic
で marks the "stage" where the action is actually being performed.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using に instead of で for action verbs (❌ 図書館に勉強します) — に is for
existence/destination (あります・います・行きます), while で is for verbs describing an activity
happening at a place.

# 7. で (means) (by/with tool, method)

Question
What does 「で (means)」mean?

Answer
By, with: indicates the tool, method, language, or means used to perform an action.

Marathi
मी busने प्रवास करतो — I travel by bus. (The instrumental suffix "ने" indicates the means of transport, directly mirroring the Japanese tool marker).

Example
Easy: バスで行きます。(バスでいきます) — I go by bus. (Why: The particle marks the vehicle as the means of transport.)
Medium: 日本語で話してください。(にほんごではなしてください) — Please speak in Japanese. (Why: The particle marks the language as the active medium of speech.)
Hard: 箸で美味しいご飯を食べましょう。(はしでおいしいごはんをたべましょう) — Let's eat delicious food with chopsticks. (Why: The particle marks the eating utensil as the physical tool of action.)

Mnemonic
で as "with/by" — picture holding the tool (バス, 箸, 日本語) that carries you through the action.

AI Notes
Common mistake: not realizing this is the SAME で as "location of action" — one particle, two jobs
depending on whether it follows a place (where) or a tool/method/language (how).

# 8. と (and, with)

Question
What does 「と」mean?

Answer
And, with: connects nouns in a complete list, or indicates a partner with whom an action is performed.

Marathi
मी मित्रासोबत खेळतो — I play with my friend. (The associative postposition "सोबत" corresponds to the relational partner function of the Japanese particle).

Example
Easy: 友達と会います。(ともだちとあいます) — I meet with my friend. (Why: The particle indicates the partner of the reciprocal action.)
Medium: 机の上に本とペンがあります。(つくえのうえにほんとペンがあります) — There are books and pens on the desk. (Why: The particle exhaustively connects two distinct objects.)
Hard: 来週の日曜日に家族と日本へ行きます。(らいしゅうのにちようびにかぞくとにほんへいきます) — Next week on Sunday, I will go to Japan with my family. (Why: The particle marks the family as the accompanying party of travel.)

Mnemonic
と links two things together like a knot — "A と B" ties them into a pair.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using と for an incomplete list — と names an EXHAUSTIVE, complete list of exactly
the items given; for "and other things," use や instead.

# 9. も (also, too)

Question
What does 「も」mean?

Answer
Also, too: indicates that the marked item shares the same property as another, replacing the particles は or を.

Marathi
मी पण भारतीय आहे — I am also Indian. (The word "पण" is the exact clitic for "also", showing a clear structural parallel with the Japanese additive marker).

Example
Easy: 私も学生です。(わたしもがくせいです) — I am also a student. (Why: The particle replaces the topic marker to show shared identity.)
Medium: 日本語も英語も難しいです。(にほんごもえいごもむずかしいです) — Both Japanese and English are difficult. (Why: Using the particle twice creates a correlative structure meaning "both... and...".)
Hard: 鈴木さんは昨日来ませんでした。今日も来ないでしょう。(すずきさんはきのうきませんでした。きょうもこないでしょう) — Mr. Suzuki didn't come yesterday. He probably won't come today either. (Why: The particle highlights that today shares the same negative outcome as yesterday.)

Mnemonic
も sounds like "mo-re of the same" — signals "this one too."

AI Notes
Common mistake: keeping は or を alongside も (❌ 私はも学生です) — も REPLACES は/が/を entirely, it
never stacks with them.

# 10. の (possessive) ('s / of)

Question
What does 「の (possessive)」mean?

Answer
Possessive marker: links nouns to show ownership, relationship, or location.

Marathi
हा माझ्या मित्राचा पेन आहे — This is my friend's pen. (The suffix "चा" represents the genitive/possessive marker, exactly matching the noun-linking structure of the Japanese particle).

Example
Easy: これは私のペンです。(これはわたしのペンです) — This is my pen. (Why: The particle connects the possessor to the possessed item.)
Medium: あれは日本の自動車ですか。(あれはにほんのじどうしゃですか) — Is that a Japanese car? (Why: The particle connects the country of origin to the product noun.)
Hard: 駅の近くに大きい本屋があります。(えきのちかくにおおきいほんやがあります) — There is a large bookstore near the station. (Why: The first particle shows a spatial relationship between the train terminal and its vicinity.)

Mnemonic
の glues two nouns together — "A の B" = "A's B."

AI Notes
Common mistake: not realizing の can chain multiple times in one phrase (駅の近くの本屋 = "the
bookstore near the station") — each の just links the next noun back to the previous one.

# 11. の (nominalizer) (turns phrase into noun)

Question
What does 「の (nominalizer)」mean?

Answer
Noun substitute: turns a verb phrase or adjective phrase into a noun clause.

Marathi
मला गाणे गाणे आवडते — I like singing songs. (The nominalized gerund verb form functions like the Japanese grammatical nominalizer to express a preference for an action).

Example
Easy: 泳ぐのが好きです。(およぐのがすきです) — I like swimming. (Why: The particle converts the dictionary verb into a noun phrase compatible with direct description.)
Medium: 日本語を話すのは面白いです。(にほんごをはなすのはおもしろいです) — Speaking Japanese is interesting. (Why: The particle turns the action phrase into the main topic of evaluation.)
Hard: 朝早く起きるのは体にいいです。(あさはやくおきるのはからだにいいです) — Waking up early in the morning is good for the body. (Why: The particle nominalizes the complex verb clause to assess its physical benefit.)

Mnemonic
の here works like English "-ing" — turning an action into a "thing" you can talk about (泳ぐ = swim
→ 泳ぐの = swimming, the activity).

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing this の with the possessive の from card 10 — context tells them apart:
after a FULL verb/adjective clause (泳ぐの), it's the nominalizer; directly between two nouns (私の
本), it's possessive.

# 12. へ (direction) (toward)

Question
What does 「へ」mean?

Answer
Toward: indicates the direction of movement toward a destination (pronounced "e").

Marathi
मी पुण्याच्या दिशेने निघालो — I headed in the direction of Pune. (The postpositional phrase "च्या दिशेने" emphasizes the direction of journey, matching the primary function of the Japanese particle).

Example
Easy: 日本へ行きます。(にほんへいきます) — I am going to Japan. (Why: The particle emphasizes the direction of travel toward the country.)
Medium: 毎朝駅へ歩いて行きます。(まいあさえきへあるいていきます) — Every morning I walk toward the station. (Why: The particle marks the spatial target of the pedestrian action.)
Hard: 友達は国へ帰って、お母さんに会います。(ともだちはくにへかえって、おかあさんにあいます) — My friend will return to their country and meet their mother. (Why: The particle indicates the physical homeland direction of departure in a multi-clause sentence.)

Mnemonic
へ (pronounced "e") points like an arrow toward a broad direction — a journey, not a pinpoint.

AI Notes
Common mistake: assuming へ and に (direction) are never interchangeable — for simple destination
movement verbs (行きます・来ます・帰ります) they're often interchangeable at N5 level; へ leans toward
the direction/journey, に leans toward the precise arrival point.

# 13. や (and (non-exhaustive list))

Question
What does 「や」mean?

Answer
And: links nouns to form an incomplete, non-exhaustive list, implying "and other things."

Marathi
मी सफरचंद आणि इतर फळे आणली — I brought apples and other fruits. (The concept of indicating representative items while implying others is expressed structurally, mirroring the Japanese listing particle).

Example
Easy: りんごやバナナを買いました。(りんごやバナナをかいました) — I bought apples and bananas (among other things). (Why: The particle implies that other items were purchased alongside the named fruits.)
Medium: カバンの中に本やペンなどがあります。(カバンのなかにほんやペンなどがあります) — There are things like books and pens in the bag. (Why: The particle is paired with another term to form a classic non-exhaustive collection.)
Hard: デパートでシャツや靴を買いたいです。(デパートでシャツやくつをかいたいです) — I want to buy shirts, shoes, and other things at the department store. (Why: The particle coordinates a representative list of targeted items of desire.)

Mnemonic
や leaves the list "open" — like saying "apples, bananas, and stuff like that."

AI Notes
Common mistake: reaching for や when the list actually IS complete — use と for a closed, exhaustive
list. や is often paired with など ("etc.") to make the open-endedness extra clear.

# 14. から (from)

Question
What does 「から」mean?

Answer
From: indicates the starting point in time, space, or a source.

Marathi
मी घरून निघालो — I started from home. (The ablative suffix "ून" indicates the point of origin, showing absolute equivalence with the Japanese starting marker).

Example
Easy: 九時から勉強します。(くじからべんきょうします) — I study from 9 o'clock. (Why: The particle marks the chronological starting point.)
Medium: ここから駅まで歩いて十分です。(ここからえきまであるいてじっぷんです) — It takes ten minutes to walk from here to the station. (Why: The particle marks the physical point of departure.)
Hard: 友達から面白い辞書をもらいました。(ともだちからおもしろいじしょをもらいました) — I received an interesting dictionary from my friend. (Why: The particle marks the personal source from whom the item was obtained.)

Mnemonic
から = "from" — like a starting gun firing "kara!" at the start line of a race.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing this から (starting point) with the OTHER から that means "because" — same
word, two different jobs: after a noun/time it's "from," after a full clause (usually ending in
です/ます) it's "because."

# 15. まで (until/to)

Question
What does 「まで」mean?

Answer
Until, to: indicates the ending point in time, space, or a physical limit.

Marathi
मी पाच वाजेपर्यंत थांबेन — I will wait until 5 o'clock. (The temporal postposition "पर्यंत" perfectly matches the boundary limit function of the Japanese particle).

Example
Easy: 五時まで働きます。(ごじまではたらきます) — I will work until 5 o'clock. (Why: The particle marks the chronological endpoint of labor.)
Medium: 東京駅から大阪駅まで何時間かかりますか。(とうきょうえきからおおさかえきまでなんじかんかかりますか) — How many hours does it take from Tokyo Station to Osaka Station? (Why: The particle indicates the physical terminus of the route.)
Hard: 晩ご飯の時間はいつも八時までです。(ばんごはんのじかんはいつもはちじまでです) — Dinner time is always up until 8 o'clock. (Why: The particle defines the absolute temporal boundary of the meal.)

Mnemonic
まで pairs naturally with から: "から...まで" = "from...to," the two bookends of a range.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using plain まで when meaning a strict deadline ("by") — for a hard deadline, までに
is used instead of plain まで (a finer nuance beyond N5, but worth flagging early to avoid confusion).

# 16. か (question marker)

Question
What does 「か」mean?

Answer
Question particle: placed at the end of a clause to turn a statement into a question or represent alternatives.

Marathi
तू आज जाणार आहेस का? — Are you going today? (The polar question clitic "का" is appended sentence-finally, mirroring the structural mechanics of the Japanese interrogative particle).

Example
Easy: あなたは学生ですか。(あなたはがくせいですか) — Are you a student? (Why: The particle turns the basic polite nominal sentence into a direct question.)
Medium: コーヒーかお茶を飲みましょう。(コーヒーかおちゃをのみましょう) — Let's drink coffee or tea. (Why: The particle connects two nouns to offer them as distinct options.)
Hard: 来週の月曜日にテストがあるかどうか分かりません。(らいしゅうのげつようびにテストがあるかどうかわかりません) — I don't know whether there is a test next Monday or not. (Why: The particle forms a double interrogative pattern to represent uncertainty.)

Mnemonic
か curls up like a little question-mark hook at the very end of the sentence.

AI Notes
Common mistake: adding a written "?" on top of か — か alone does the job in Japanese (the question
mark is optional/informal). Also remember か between two nouns (AかB) means "A or B" — a second
common function worth keeping straight from the sentence-ending one.
