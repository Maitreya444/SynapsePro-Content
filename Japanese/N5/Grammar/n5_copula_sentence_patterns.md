# G017: Noun + です
Question
What does 「Noun + です」mean?

Answer
Polite copula meaning "to be" (am/is/are) used to declare identity or state.

Marathi
... आहे (Noun + aahe) — is/am/are. For example: तो विद्यार्थी आहे (To vidyarthi aahe) — He is a student. In this tongue, the verb 'आहे' comes at the end, perfectly matching the SOV pattern of Japanese where 'です' concludes the sentence.

Example
Easy: これは本です。(これ は ほん です) — This is a book. (Why: Standard use of the polite copula to identify an object.)
Medium: 私の弟は学生です。(わたし の おとうと は がくせい です) — My younger brother is a student. (Why: Demonstrates the copula declaring the status of a third-person subject.)
Hard: あの赤い車は山田さんのです。(あの あかい くるま は やまださん の です) — That red car is Mr. Yamada's. (Why: Shows the copula concluding a sentence where the noun after the possessive marker 'の' is omitted.)

Mnemonic
です is a polite little "yes/is" stamp placed at the very end of a sentence.

AI Notes
Common mistake: attaching です directly onto a verb to make it polite (❌ 食べますです) — です only
pairs with nouns/na-adjectives for "is/am/are"; verbs already carry their own politeness via ます.

# G018: Noun + ではありません / じゃないです
Question
What does 「Noun + ではありません / じゃないです」mean?

Answer
Polite negative copula meaning "is not", "am not", or "are not".

Marathi
... नाही (Noun + nahi) — is not / am not / are not. For example: तो विद्यार्थी नाही (To vidyarthi nahi) — He is not a student. Both Japanese (ではありません) and the corresponding Devanagari phrase (नाही) conclude sentences with the negative copula.

Example
Easy: 私は先生ではありません。(わたし は せんせい ではありません) — I am not a teacher. (Why: Simple polite negation of a noun using the formal ではありません.)
Medium: 今日は日曜日じゃないです。(きょう は にちようび じゃないです) — Today is not Sunday. (Why: Demonstrates the more conversational yet polite negative form 'じゃないです' with a day of the week.)
Hard: あの人はこの学校の学生ではありません。(あの ひと は この がっこう の がくせい ではありません) — That person is not a student of this school. (Why: Negates a noun that is modified by a relative genitive phrase.)

Mnemonic
では = "as for that" + ありません = "doesn't exist" → "as for that, it doesn't exist [as that]."

AI Notes
Common mistake: assuming じゃないです is less polite than ではありません — both are equally polite;
じゃ is just the natural spoken contraction of では, fine in everyday polite conversation.

# G019: Noun + でした
Question
What does 「Noun + でした」mean?

Answer
Polite past tense of the copula です, meaning "was" or "were".

Marathi
... होता/होती (Noun + hota/hoti) — was/were. For example: काल सुट्टी होती (Kaal sutti hoti) — Yesterday was a holiday. Just like Japanese 'でした', the local past tense copula is placed at the end of the sentence.

Example
Easy: 昨日は雨でした。(きのう は あめ でした) — Yesterday was rainy. (Why: Uses でした to make a simple past polite statement about a noun (weather).)
Medium: 先週は休みでした。(せんしゅう は やすみ でした) — Last week was a day off. (Why: Employs でした with a past time indicator to describe a past state.)
Hard: 昨日のテストはとても簡単でした。(きのう の てすと は とても かんたん でした) — Yesterday's test was very easy. (Why: Applies でした to a past state described by a な-adjective base.)

Mnemonic
でした = です + た (the past marker) — "was-desu," the past tense riding piggyback on です.

AI Notes
Common mistake: tacking でした onto an already-past verb (❌ 食べましたでした) — でした only attaches
to nouns/na-adjectives; verbs carry their own past tense via ました.

# G020: Noun + ではありませんでした
Question
What does 「Noun + ではありませんでした」mean?

Answer
Polite past negative copula meaning "was not" or "were not".

Marathi
... नव्हता/नव्हती (Noun + navhta/navhti) — was not / were not. For example: काल सुट्टी नव्हती (Kaal sutti navhti) — Yesterday was not a holiday. The local past negative copula occupies the final position, echoing the structure of 'ではありませんでした'.

Example
Easy: 昨日は休みではありませんでした。(きのう は やすみ ではありませんでした) — Yesterday was not a day off. (Why: baseline demonstration of past polite negative conjugation for a noun.)
Medium: そのテストは簡単ではありませんでした。(その てすと は かんたん ではありませんでした) — That test was not easy. (Why: Negates the past state of a な-adjective (簡単).)
Hard: 先週の金曜日は雨ではありませんでした。(せんしゅう の 金ようび は あめ ではありませんでした) — Last Friday was not rainy. (Why: Connects a past time noun modifier with the formal past negative copula.)

Mnemonic
Stack all three pieces: では (as for) + ありません (doesn't exist) + でした (past) — "as for that, it
didn't exist [as that], back then."

AI Notes
Common mistake: thinking the shorter じゃなかったです is a separate pattern — it's the same meaning,
just the casual-but-polite version, freely interchangeable with ではありませんでした.

# G021: Xは Yです
Question
What does 「Xは Yです」mean?

Answer
The basic sentence structure meaning "As for X, it is Y" (X is Y).

Marathi
X, Y आहे (X, Y aahe) — X is Y. For example: मी शिक्षक आहे (Mi shikshak aahe) — I am a teacher. This represents an identical SOV alignment: Subject (X) + Object/Complement (Y) + Copula (आहे).

Example
Easy: 私は学生です。(わたし は がくせい です) — I am a student. (Why: The most fundamental template of the Xは Yです pattern.)
Medium: 山田さんはこの会社の社員です。(やまださん は この かいしゃ の しゃいん です) — Mr. Yamada is an employee of this company. (Why: Expands the Y component with a noun modifying particle 'の'.)
Hard: 私の趣味は日本の映画を見ることです。(わたし の しゅみ は にほん の えいが を みる こと です) — My hobby is watching Japanese movies. (Why: Nominalizes a verb phrase using 'ること' to fit into the Y noun slot.)

Mnemonic
は marks "as for X..." and です seals "...it's Y" — read the whole sentence as one breath: "As for X,
Y."

AI Notes
Common mistake: reaching for が instead of は here — for a simple "X is Y" statement, は is almost
always right; が would instead emphasize X as the specific answer to "who/what is Y?"

# G022: Xは Yですか
Question
What does 「Xは Yですか」mean?

Answer
The polite question form of "Is X Y?", created by adding the question particle か to です.

Marathi
X, Y आहे का? (X, Y aahe ka?) — Is X Y? For example: तू विद्यार्थी आहेस का? (Tu vidyarthi aahes ka?) — Are you a student? The local question marker 'का' is placed at the absolute end, perfectly matching the position and function of 'か'.

Example
Easy: あなたは学生ですか。(あなた は がくせい ですか) — Are you a student? (Why: Standard baseline question structure using the topic marker and ending question particle.)
Medium: あの人は山田さんの先生ですか。(あの ひと は やまださん の せんせい ですか) — Is that person over there Mr. Yamada's teacher? (Why: Integrates demonstratives and possessives inside a polite question frame.)
Hard: この赤い辞書はあなたのですか。(この あかい じしょ は あなた の ですか) — Is this red dictionary yours? (Why: Forms a question about ownership using a possessive pronoun 'あなた の' before the question copula.)

Mnemonic
か is a tiny hook curling up at the end of the sentence, like a question mark riding on です.

AI Notes
Common mistake: adding a written "?" on top of か — か alone marks the question in Japanese, so the
question mark is redundant (common informally, but not needed). Word order otherwise stays exactly
statement-like.
