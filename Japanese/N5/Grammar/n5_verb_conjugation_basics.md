# G023: ます-form (present/future, affirmative)

Question
What does 「ます-form (present/future, affirmative)」mean?

Answer
Polite present or future affirmative form used to express habits, general truths, or future intentions.

Marathi
मी रोज अभ्यास करतो (मी रोज अभ्यास करते) — I study every day. Both languages are SOV and place the polite verb form at the absolute end of the sentence to show respect.

Example
Easy: 毎日日本語を勉強します。 (まいにちにほんごをべんきょうします。) — I study Japanese every day. (Why: Explains a habitual daily action using the polite present form.)
Medium: 明日友達と図書館に行きます。 (あしたともだちとしょかんじょにいきます。) — I will go to the library with my friend tomorrow. (Why: Marks a future intention scheduled for tomorrow with a target destination.)
Hard: 毎週日曜日にデパートで買い物をします。 (まいしゅうにちようびにでぱーとでかいものをします。) — I do shopping at the department store every Sunday. (Why: Combines a recurring weekly time marker, location of action, and a compound verb.)

Mnemonic
ます is the "polish" you put on any verb to make it presentable in public — plain 食べる becomes
polite 食べます.

AI Notes
Common mistake: keeping ます-form on a verb embedded before a grammar pattern that needs the plain
form instead (e.g. ことができます needs 食べること, not 食べますこと) — ます belongs on the final verb
of a polite sentence, not mid-sentence.

# G024: ません (present/future, negative)

Question
What does 「ません (present/future, negative)」mean?

Answer
Polite present or future negative form meaning "do not" or "will not."

Marathi
मी चहा पीत नाही — I do not drink tea. Just as Marathi places the negative auxiliary "नाही" at the end of the clause, Japanese uses "ません" to negate the verb sentence-finally.

Example
Easy: お酒を飲みません。 (おさけをのみません。) — I do not drink alcohol. (Why: States a personal habit or general rule in the polite negative.)
Medium: 今日はテレビを見ません。 (きょうはてれびをみません。) — I will not watch TV today. (Why: Indicates a specific negative plan or choice for the current day.)
Hard: 私はその漢字がよく分かりません。 (わたしはそのかんじがよくわかりません。) — I do not understand those kanji characters well. (Why: Negates the intransitive verb of understanding, modified by the adverb "well.")

Mnemonic
Swap す for せん — ます becomes ません, like flipping a switch off.

AI Notes
Common mistake: piling on an extra ない somewhere too (❌ 飲みませんない) — ません is already the
complete negative; there's nothing left to negate again.

# G025: ました (past, affirmative)

Question
What does 「ました (past, affirmative)」mean?

Answer
Polite past affirmative form meaning "did."

Marathi
मी काल पत्र लिहिले — I wrote a letter yesterday. In both languages, the verb conjugates into the past tense and sits at the very end of the sentence.

Example
Easy: 昨日買い物をしました。 (きのうかいものをしました。) — I went shopping yesterday. (Why: Highlights a completed action in the past using a standard time adverb.)
Medium: 今朝七時に起きました。 (けさしちじにおきました。) — I woke up at seven o'clock this morning. (Why: Pairs a past action with a specific time point marked by the particle に.)
Hard: 先週の日曜日に友達に会いました。 (せんしゅうのにちようびにともだちにあいました。) — I met my friend last Sunday. (Why: Demonstrates a past event with a specific target person marked by the particle に.)

Mnemonic
ます → ました, just like English adds "-ed" for past tense.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using でした on a verb instead of ました (❌ 食べるでした) — でした is only for
nouns/な-adjectives; verbs always take ました for the polite past.

# G026: ませんでした (past, negative)

Question
What does 「ませんでした (past, negative)」mean?

Answer
Polite past negative form meaning "did not."

Marathi
मी अभ्यास केला नाही — I did not study. Both languages place the negative past inflected markers at the end of the sentence.

Example
Easy: 朝ご飯を食べませんでした。 (あすごはんをたべませんでした。) — I did not eat breakfast. (Why: Indicates a simple non-event in the past morning.)
Medium: 昨日はどこへも行きませんでした。 (きのうはどこへもいきませんでした。) — I did not go anywhere yesterday. (Why: Uses the complete negative location phrase "どこへも" with past negation.)
Hard: 先週は忙しくて本を読みませんでした。 (せんしゅうはいそがしくてほんをよみませんでした。) — Last week I was busy and did not read books. (Why: Connects a past reason in the て-form of an adjective with a final negative past verb.)

Mnemonic
Combine both pieces: ません (negative) + でした (past) = "didn't."

AI Notes
Common mistake: slipping in plain-form だった (❌ ませんだった) — the polite past negative is always
ませんでした, never mixed with a plain-form ending.

# G027: て-form formation

Question
What does 「て-form formation」mean?

Answer
The conjunctive form of verbs used to connect clauses, list sequential actions, or attach grammatical helpers.

Marathi
मी उठून चहा पितो — I wake up and drink tea. The Marathi suffix "-ऊन" (e.g. उठून - having woken up) is a perfect syntactic parallel to the Japanese て-form for linking actions consecutively.

Example
Easy: 朝起きて、水を飲みます。 (あさおきて、みずをのみます。) — I wake up in the morning and drink water. (Why: Uses the て-form of 起きる to link two actions chronologically.)
Medium: 図書館に行って、本を読みました。 (としょかんにいって、ほんをよみました。) — I went to the library and read a book. (Why: Shows a series of completed past actions linked using the て-form of 行く.)
Hard: 毎晩お風呂に入ってから寝ます。 (まいばんおふろにはいってからねます。) — Every night I take a bath and then sleep. (Why: Pairs the て-form with から to emphasize that one action happens strictly after another.)

Mnemonic
Think of て as a connector hook linking one action to the next, like a chain link between clauses.

AI Notes
Common mistake: applying the wrong sound-shift rule for group-1 (u-verb) て-forms (う/つ/る→って,
む/ぬ/ぶ→んで, く→いて, ぐ→いで, す→して) — these are grouped by the verb's final sound, not
guessable, so they need direct memorization.

# G028: ~てください

Question
What does 「~てください」mean?

Answer
A polite request meaning "please do this action."

Marathi
कृपया पुस्तक उघडा — Please open the book. Both languages utilize a polite imperative ending (Marathi "-ा" ending and Japanese "てください") to request action.

Example
Easy: 本を読んでください。 (ほんをよんでください。) — Please read the book. (Why: Requests a basic action using the て-form of 読む.)
Medium: ここに名前を書いてください。 (ここになまえをかいてください。) — Please write your name here. (Why: Directs a polite request to a specific location marked by the particle に.)
Hard: すみませんが、ゆっくり話してください。 (すみませんが、ゆっくりはなしてください。) — Excuse me, but please speak slowly. (Why: Combines a polite sentence opener with an adverb modifying the requested action.)

Mnemonic
て-form + ください = "please give [me the action]" — literally handing over the て-form as a request.

AI Notes
Common mistake: attaching ください straight to the dictionary form or ます-form (❌ 読むください, ❌
読みますください) — it must always follow the て-form (読んでください).

# G029: ~ています (progressive)

Question
What does 「~ています (progressive)」mean?

Answer
The progressive state indicating that an action is currently ongoing ("is doing").

Marathi
मी अभ्यास करत आहे — I am studying. Marathi uses the helper verb "आहे" with the continuous participle "करत", structurally paralleling the て-form + います helper.

Example
Easy: テレビを見ています。 (てれびをみています。) — I am watching TV. (Why: Expresses a standard active process occurring at the present moment.)
Medium: 彼は今日本語を勉強しています。 (かれはいまにほんごをべんきょうしています。) — He is studying Japanese right now. (Why: Combines an ongoing continuous activity with a specific subject and time modifier.)
Hard: 外で雨が降っています。 (そとであめがふっています。) — It is raining outside. (Why: Shows an ongoing natural phenomenon marked by the subject marker が.)

Mnemonic
て-form + います = "doing-and-existing-right-now" — picture the action frozen mid-motion.

AI Notes
Common mistake: assuming ています always means "in progress" — for instantaneous change-of-state
verbs (結婚する, 住む, 知る) it instead describes a resulting STATE, not an ongoing process (see the
next card).

# G030: ~ています (state/habitual)

Question
What does 「~ています (state/habitual)」mean?

Answer
Expresses a stable state resulting from a past action, or a long-term habitual action such as occupation or residence.

Marathi
मी मुंबईत राहत आहे — I live in Mumbai. Both languages use their progressive structures to indicate a continuous state of residence or occupation rather than a momentary action.

Example
Easy: 東京に住んでいます。 (とうきょうにすんでいます。) — I live in Tokyo. (Why: Denotes a stable state of living in a specific city marked by に.)
Medium: 私は結婚しています。 (わたしはけっこんしています。) — I am married. (Why: Demonstrates a permanent state resulting from the past action of getting married.)
Hard: 姉は大学で教えています。 (あねはだいがくでおしえています。) — My older sister teaches at a university. (Why: Shows a habitual professional occupation using the state function at a location marked by で.)

Mnemonic
Same て-form + います, but for "instant-change" verbs it reads as "the result is still true," not "in
the middle of happening."

AI Notes
Common mistake: translating 結婚しています as "I am getting married" (in progress) instead of "I am
married" (resulting state) — momentary/change-of-state verbs flip ています from progressive to
stative meaning.

# G031: ~てもいいです

Question
What does 「~てもいいです」mean?

Answer
Expresses permission meaning "may" or "it is okay to do."

Marathi
तू आत आला तरी चालेल — It is okay even if you come in. The Marathi phrasing "आला तरी चालेल" (even if you do X, it is fine) mirrors the concessive syntax of the て-form + も (even) + いいです (is good).

Example
Easy: 入ってもいいです。 (はいってもいいです。) — You may enter. (Why: Grants permission to perform a simple movement.)
Medium: ここで写真を撮ってもいいですか。 (ここでしゃしんをとってもいいですか。) — May I take pictures here? (Why: Formulates a polite question asking for permission to do an action at a location.)
Hard: 暑いので、窓を開けてもいいですか。 (あついので、まどをあけてもいいですか。) — Since it is hot, may I open the window? (Why: Prefaces a request for permission with an adjective clause explaining the physical reason.)

Mnemonic
て-form + も (even) + いいです (is good) = "even if [you do this], it's fine."

AI Notes
Common mistake: dropping the か when actually asking for permission — てもいいです alone is a
statement of general permission; てもいいですか is the real question "may I?"

# G032: ~てはいけません

Question
What does 「~てはいけません」mean?

Answer
Expresses a formal prohibition meaning "must not" or "is not allowed to."

Marathi
तिथे कचरा टाकू नका (तिथे कचरा टाकण्यास मनाई आहे) — You must not throw garbage there. Marathi uses "मनाई आहे" or negative imperatives to convey prohibition at the end of the sentence.

Example
Easy: ここで泳いではいけません。 (ここでおよいではいけません。) — You must not swim here. (Why: Forbids an action in a specified location.)
Medium: 教室の中で歌ってはいけません。 (きょうしつのなかでうたってはいけません。) — You must not sing inside the classroom. (Why: Sets a strict behavioral rule within a specific compound space.)
Hard: 危ないですから、そこに入ってはいけません。 (あぶないですから、そこにはいってはいけません。) — Since it is dangerous, you must not enter there. (Why: Links a cause using the explanation marker ですから with a direct spatial prohibition.)

Mnemonic
て-form + は (as for) + いけません (no good) = "as for doing this — no good."

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing てはいけません (formal, rule-like prohibition) with ないでください (a
softer personal request not to do something) — てはいけません sounds like a posted rule or law, not
a casual ask.

# G033: Dictionary form (plain, present)

Question
What does 「Dictionary form (plain, present)」mean?

Answer
The basic, uninflected form of verbs used in casual speech or as modifiers before certain nouns.

Marathi
तो रोज धावतो — He runs every day. Both languages utilize the plain, basic verb form to express habitual actions or future predictions in casual settings.

Example
Easy: 毎日走る。 (まいにちはしる。) — I run every day. (Why: Showcases a habitual plain present verb in casual speech.)
Medium: 明日友達に会う。 (あしたともだちにあう。) — I will meet my friend tomorrow. (Why: Predicts a future event casually using the dictionary form.)
Hard: 私は来年日本に行くつもりです。 (わたしはらいねんにほんにおいくつもりです。) — I plan to go to Japan next year. (Why: Embeds the dictionary form before the auxiliary noun "つもり" to express future intent.)

Mnemonic
The dictionary form is the verb's "ID photo" — the exact headword you'd look it up by, no
conjugation makeup applied.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using the dictionary form as the main verb in polite conversation — it's for casual
speech or as a base before certain grammar patterns (ことができます, つもりです); a polite sentence's
final verb still needs ます.

# G034: ない-form (plain, negative)

Question
What does 「ない-form (plain, negative)」mean?

Answer
The plain negative form of verbs meaning "do not" or "will not" in casual context or grammar patterns.

Marathi
तो काम करत नाही — He does not work. Reflects the casual negative present tense, where the main verb carries the negative element at the end of the sentence.

Example
Easy: 今日は食べない。 (きょうはたべない。) — I will not eat today. (Why: Expresses a basic future refusal or choice in casual speech.)
Medium: お金がない。 (おかねがない。) — There is no money. (Why: Uses the plain negative irregular verb of existence to state a condition.)
Hard: 時間がないので、走る。 (じかんがないので、はしる。) — Since there is no time, I will run. (Why: Couples a plain negative reason clause using ので with a plain present verb.)

Mnemonic
ない tacks onto the verb's negative stem — think "nah, I won't" (na-i).

AI Notes
Common mistake: mixing up group-1 (u-verb) and group-2 (る-verb) ない-form rules — group 2 just drops
る and adds ない (食べる→食べない), but group 1 shifts the final vowel to "a" first (飲む→飲まない). Mixing
these two patterns up is one of the most common N5 conjugation errors.

# G035: Verb stem + たいです

Question
What does 「Verb stem + たいです」mean?

Answer
Expresses the speaker's desire to perform a specific action ("want to do").

Marathi
मला पाणी प्यायचे आहे — I want to drink water. The Marathi structure places the desired action first followed by "पाहिजे" or "इच्छा आहे", matching the placement of standard verbal intent markers in Japanese.

Example
Easy: 水が飲みたいです。 (みずがのみたいです。) — I want to drink water. (Why: Expresses a basic physical desire, optionally using が with the たい form.)
Medium: 日本で寿司を食べたいです。 (にほんですしをたべたいです。) — I want to eat sushi in Japan. (Why: Combines a location marker and direct object with a stated wish.)
Hard: 休みの日にはどこへも行きたくないです。 (やすみのひにはどこへもいきたくないです。) — On my days off, I don't want to go anywhere. (Why: Integrates an negative shift of the たい form to express a total absence of desire to go anywhere.)

Mnemonic
たい sounds like "tie" — "I want to tie myself to doing this action."

AI Notes
Common mistake: forgetting たいです is strictly for the SPEAKER's own desire (or asking the listener's,
in a question) — describing a third person's desire needs たがっている instead, which is beyond N5
but worth knowing exists. Also note the object particle often shifts from を to が with たい (水が
飲みたい), though を remains acceptable.
