# G044
Question
What does 「があります」mean?

Answer
There is / are (used to show the existence of inanimate, non-living things).

Marathi
टेबलावर पुस्तक आहे (Teblavar pustak aahe) — There is a book on the table. Both Japanese and Marathi are SOV languages where the existence verb (あります / आहे) is placed at the very end of the sentence, immediately following the subject.

Example
Easy: 机の上に本があります。(つくえのうえにほんがあります) — There is a book on the desk. (Why: The subject "本" is an inanimate object, so the existence verb is used)
Medium: この教室に新しいパソコンがあります。(このきょうしつにあたらしいぱするとんがあります) — There is a new computer in this classroom. (Why: Introduces a location modifier and an adjective describing the inanimate subject)
Hard: 駅の近くに大きい病院がありますが、とても便利です。(えきのちかくにおおきいびょういんがありますが、とてもべんりです) — There is a big hospital near the station, and it is very convenient. (Why: Integrates inanimate existence in a complex compound sentence joined by the adversative conjunction "が")

Mnemonic
あります sounds like a little "ah, it's there!" of discovery — use it for anything that can't get up
and walk away on its own.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using います for inanimate objects (❌ 本がいます) — Japanese enforces a strict
animate/inanimate split that English "there is" ignores. Quick check: can it move on its own? If
not, あります.

# G045
Question
What does 「がいます」mean?

Answer
There is / are (used to show the existence of animate, living beings such as people and animals).

Marathi
खोलीत मुलगा आहे (Kholit mulga aahe) — There is a boy in the room. In both languages, the existence of living subjects is marked with the terminal verb (います / आहे) following the marked location and subject.

Example
Easy: 庭に犬がいます。(にわにいぬがいます) — There is a dog in the garden. (Why: "犬" is a living animal, so the animate existence verb is mandatory)
Medium: あそこに有名な先生がいます。(あそこにゆうめいなせんせいがいます) — There is a famous teacher over there. (Why: Links a distant location "あそこ" with a human subject modified by a な-adjective)
Hard: 私の家には可愛い猫が三匹います。(わたしのいえにはかわいいねこがさんびきいます) — There are three cute cats in my house. (Why: Combines a location-topic compound "には", an adjective, and a specific N5 animal counter "三匹")

Mnemonic
います is for anyone who can walk (or crawl, or fly) into the room on their own — people and animals
only.

AI Notes
Common mistake: assuming "alive" is enough to trigger います — plants are alive but still take
あります, since the real rule is about self-directed movement, not biology. Also remember animals
usually need a species-specific counter (匹 for small animals, 頭 for large ones) when giving a
quantity.

# G046
Question
What does 「~があります」mean when used for possession?

Answer
I have / someone has (used to express ownership of tangible items, or availability of time/events).

Marathi
माझ्याजवळ पैसे आहेत (Mazyajaval paise aahet) — I have money. Marathi expresses possession using the postposition "जवळ" (near/with) combined with an existence verb, which mirrors the spatial concept of the Japanese topic-subject-existence structure.

Example
Easy: 私はお金があります。(わたしはおかねがあります) — I have money. (Why: The basic possession structure where the possessor is the topic and the possessed item is marked with "が")
Medium: 今日は忙しいですから、時間がありません。(きょうはいそがしいですから、じかんがありません) — I don't have time today because I am busy. (Why: Expresses the negative state of possessing an abstract asset, "time", in a causal sentence)
Hard: 来週の日曜日に大切な用事があります。(らいしゅうのにちようびにたいせつなようじがあります) — I have an important errand next Sunday. (Why: Uses the possession construction for abstract obligations, integrating date nouns and い-adjectives)

Mnemonic
Same あります as existence — just reframed. "Near me, [thing] exists" is Japanese's way of saying
"I have [thing]." One verb, two readings.

AI Notes
Common mistake: marking the possessed item with を instead of が — possession with あります always
uses が, because grammatically the thing is treated as "existing" for the possessor (marked by は),
not as something being acted upon.
