# Card G056: ~前に

Question
What does 「~前に」mean?

Answer
Before doing a verb (dictionary form + 前に) or before a noun (noun + の前に).

Marathi
जेवण्यापूर्वी हात धुवा — Wash hands before eating. The Japanese verb dictionary form + 前に (mae ni) structurally parallels the Marathi verbal suffix '-ण्यापूर्वी' (nyapurvi).

Example
Easy: 寝る前に本を読みます。(ねる まえに ほんを よみます。) — I read a book before sleeping. (Why: The dictionary verb 寝る is attached to 前に to mark the prior event.)
Medium: 食事の前に手を洗ってください。(しょくじの まえに てを あらってください。) — Please wash your hands before meals. (Why: Shows noun + の前に pattern combined with a polite request.)
Hard: 日本へ来る前に日本語を勉強しました。(にほんへ くる まえに にほんごを べんきょうしました。) — I studied Japanese before coming to Japan. (Why: Expresses a past action done prior to a destination-based motion verb.)

Mnemonic
前 means "front/before" — literally "in front of [the verb happening]."

AI Notes
Common mistake: using the past-tense verb before 前に (❌ 寝た前に) — 前に always takes the DICTIONARY
form of the verb, even inside an otherwise past-tense sentence, since 前に itself expresses "before,"
not tense.

# Card G057: ~後で

Question
What does 「~後で」mean?

Answer
After doing a verb (past plain た-form + 後で) or after a noun (noun + の後で).

Marathi
जेवल्यानंतर औषध घ्या — Take medicine after eating. The past tense verb + 後で (ato de) structurally aligns with the Marathi verb participle ending in '-ल्यानंतर' (lyananatar).

Example
Easy: ご飯を食べた後で薬を飲みます。(ごはんを たべた あとで くすりを のみます。) — I take medicine after eating a meal. (Why: The past plain た-form of 食べる is connected to 後で.)
Medium: 仕事の後で飲みに行きましょう。(しごとの あとで のみに いきましょう。) — Let's go for a drink after work. (Why: Demonstrates noun + の後で linked with a cooperative volitional invitation.)
Hard: 映画を見た後で友達とお茶を飲みました。(えいがを みた あとで ともだちと おちゃを のみました。) — After watching the movie, I drank tea with my friend. (Why: Integrates past action sequence with particle と for companion action.)

Mnemonic
後 means "after/behind," で adds "at that point" — "at the point after [X]."

AI Notes
Common mistake: using the dictionary form before 後で (❌ 食べる後で) — unlike 前に, 後で requires the
PAST-plain (た-form) of the verb, since the first action must already be completed.

# Card G058: ~とき

Question
What does 「~とき」mean?

Answer
When... / At the time of... (used to connect two clauses indicating time of occurrence).

Marathi
मी लहान असताना खेळायचो — When I was a child, I used to play. The Japanese noun/verb modification before とき (toki) directly matches the Marathi clause-ending '-तेव्हा' (tevha) or 'वेळी' (veli).

Example
Easy: 暇なときテレビを見ます。(ひまな とき てれびを みます。) — I watch TV when I am free. (Why: Shows a な-adjective modifying とき directly.)
Medium: 図書館で本を借りるときカードが要ります。(としょかんで ほんを かりるとき かーどが いります。) — You need a card when borrowing books from the library. (Why: Uses verb dictionary form to define a situational requirement clause.)
Hard: 子どものときよく川で泳ぎました。(こどもの とき よく かわで およぎました。) — When I was a child, I swam in the river often. (Why: Illustrates noun + のとき with a past frequency narrative.)

Mnemonic
とき literally means "time" — "verb/adjective + とき" = "at the time when [X]."

AI Notes
Common mistake: mismatching tense inside the とき clause — the verb's tense before とき matters:
食べるとき = "when [about to] eat," 食べたとき = "when [already] ate/after eating." Mixing these up is
a very common N5 slip.

# Card G059: ~から (because, conjunction)

Question
What does 「~から (because, conjunction)」mean?

Answer
Because / Since (placed at the end of the reason clause to explain cause and effect).

Marathi
पाऊस पडत आहे म्हणून मी जाणार नाही — It is raining, so I will not go. The Japanese reason clause ending in から (kara) maps closely to the Marathi logical connector 'म्हणून' (mhanun).

Example
Easy: 暑いから窓を開けます。(あついから まどを あけます。) — I will open the window because it is hot. (Why: Connects a simple い-adjective description as the cause.)
Medium: 時間がありませんからタクシーで行きます。(じかんが ありませんから たくしーで いきます。) — I will go by taxi because I do not have time. (Why: Uses a polite negative verb phrase as the reason before a means of transport.)
Hard: 明日試験がありますから今晩勉強しなければなりません。(あした しけんが ありますから こんばん べんきょう しなければ なりません。) — Because I have an exam tomorrow, I must study tonight. (Why: Links an existential reason with a high-level obligation construct.)

Mnemonic
から attaches right after the reason, like a signpost saying "because of THIS."

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing this から (reason) with the OTHER から meaning "from" (starting point) —
after a full clause (usually ending です/ます) it means "because"; after a noun/time, it means "from."

# Card G060: 一つ、二つ… (general counter)

Question
What does 「一つ、二つ…」mean?

Answer
The universal, native Japanese counter system used for counting general 3D items from one to ten.

Marathi
मला एक आंबा द्या — Give me one mango. Unlike Marathi which uses cardinal numbers directly, Japanese requires native numerical counters for physical items.

Example
Easy: りんごを一つください。(りんごを ひとつ ください。) — One apple, please. (Why: Demonstrates standard general counter placement following the noun.)
Medium: みかんを三つと卵を四つ買いました。(みかんを みっつと たまごを よっつ かいました。) — I bought three mandarins and four eggs. (Why: Coordinates multiple general counts using linking particles.)
Hard: このテーブルに椅子がいくつありますか。(この てーぶるに いすが いくつ ありますか。) — How many chairs are there at this table? (Why: Tests the interrogative counterpart 'いくつ' in an existential setting.)

Mnemonic
The つ-counter series follows the old native number rhythm (ひとつ、ふたつ、みっつ、よっつ…) — sing it
like a nursery-rhyme count, since it only goes up to とお (10).

AI Notes
Common mistake: trying to use the regular numbers (いち、に、さん) directly with つ (❌ いちつ) — the
つ-counter series has its own irregular readings that must be memorized separately from standard
number readings.

# Card G061: 人 counter (~人)

Question
What does 「人 counter (~人)」mean?

Answer
The counter suffix used for counting people (notably 1 person is ひとり, 2 people is ふたり, and 3+ is ~にん).

Marathi
वर्गात पाच विद्यार्थी आहेत — There are five students in the classroom. The counter suffix 人 (~nin) acts like a noun classifier following the number.

Example
Easy: 教室に学生が五人います。(きょうしつに がくせいが ごにん います。) — There are five students in the classroom. (Why: Displays people counter placement in an existential sentence with います.)
Medium: 私の家族は三人です。(わたしの かぞくは さんにん です。) — There are three people in my family. (Why: Defines a collective count using the polite copula.)
Hard: 昨日一人で映画館へ行きました。(きのう ひとりで えいがかんへ いきました。) — I went to the movie theater alone yesterday. (Why: Shows the irregular single person counter form '一人で' acting adverbially.)

Mnemonic
1 and 2 are special (ひとり, ふたり); everything from 3 onward just adds にん (さんにん, よにん…) —
"the first two break the rule, the rest follow it."

AI Notes
Common mistake: saying いちにん/ににん for 1/2 people instead of the irregular ひとり/ふたり — these two
exceptions are among the most tested N5 counter forms.

# Card G062: 匹/頭 counter (animals)

Question
What does 「匹/頭 counter (animals)」mean?

Answer
Counters for animals: ~匹 (ひき) is for small animals/fish/insects, while ~頭 (とう) is for large animals like cows/horses.

Marathi
माझ्याकडे दोन कुत्रे आहेत — I have two dogs. Japanese distinguishes counts based on animal size using distinct suffixes.

Example
Easy: 犬が一匹います。(いぬが いっぴき います。) — There is one dog. (Why: Shows animal counter with phonetic change 'いっぴき' for one small animal.)
Medium: 池に魚が三匹泳いでいます。(いけに さかなが さんびき およいでいます。) — Three fish are swimming in the pond. (Why: Illustrates plural small animal counts with progressive verb action.)
Hard: あの動物園にはゾウが二頭います。(あの どうぶつえんには ぞうが にとう います。) — There are two elephants in that zoo. (Why: Tests the large animal counter '頭' for elephants.)

Mnemonic
匹 (small, scurrying — "hiki") vs 頭 (big — "tou," think "tall," for elephants and cows).

AI Notes
Common mistake: forgetting 匹's irregular sound shifts with certain numbers (一匹=いっぴき, 三匹=さんびき,
六匹=ろっぴき) — these gemination/rendaku changes don't follow a simple rule and need direct
memorization per number.

The counter suffix ~枚 (まい) used for thin, flat objects like paper, plates, stamps, or shirts.

दोन तिकीट द्या — Give two tickets. Japanese groups flat, two-dimensional items under the classifier suffix.

# Card G064: ぐらい/くらい

Question
What does 「ぐらい/くらい」mean?

Answer
Approximately / About (placed immediately after quantities, numbers, or time periods).

Marathi
मी येथे दोन तास थांबलो — I stayed here for about two hours. The post-positional 'ぐらい' (gurai) matches Marathi approximate indicators placed near counts.

Example
Easy: 三十万円ぐらいのパソコンを買いました。(さんじゅうまんえん ぐらいの ぱそこんを かいました。) — I bought a computer worth about 300,000 yen. (Why: Connects approximate price to a noun using の.)
Medium: 毎日一時間半くらい走ります。(まいにち いちじかんはん くらい はしります。) — I run for about one and a half hours every day. (Why: Expresses approximate temporal duration with an active verb.)
Hard: うちから駅まで歩いて十分ぐらいかかります。(うちから えきまで あるいて じゅっぷん ぐらい かかります。) — It takes about ten minutes to walk from my home to the station. (Why: Integrates space boundaries, means, and approximate transit time.)

Mnemonic
ぐらい softens a number like adding "-ish" in English — "10分ぐらい" = "10 minutes-ish."

AI Notes
Common mistake: using ごろ instead of ぐらい/くらい — ごろ is for an approximate POINT in time (7時ごろ
= "around 7 o'clock"), while ぐらい/くらい is for an approximate AMOUNT or DURATION (7時間ぐらい =
"about 7 hours"). Mixing these two up is a classic N5 error.

# Card G065: だけ

Question
What does 「だけ」mean?

Answer
Only / Just (placed after nouns or counts to specify exclusivity in affirmative sentences).

Marathi
मी फक्त पाणी पिणार — I will drink only water. Both systems place focus on the limited noun, though Marathi 'फक्त' typically precedes while 'だけ' follows the noun.

Example
Easy: 水だけ飲みます。(みずだけ のみます。) — I drink only water. (Why: Emphasizes a singular noun choice in the affirmative.)
Medium: 五分だけ待ってください。(ごふんだけ まってください。) — Please wait for just five minutes. (Why: Restricts a request to a small, specific time limit.)
Hard: 私はひらがなだけ書くことができます。(わたしは ひらがなだけ かく ことができます。) — I can write only hiragana. (Why: Combines the restrictive marker with the dictionary potential pattern.)

Mnemonic
だけ sits right after the thing being limited, like slapping an "only-this" sticker on it.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing だけ (only — neutral, works fine in affirmative sentences) with しか~ない
(only — implies "sadly just this much," always needs a negative verb). だけ doesn't require
negation; しか does.

# Card G066: しか~ない

Question
What does 「しか~ない」mean?

Answer
Only / Nothing but (requires a negative verb at the end; implies the limit is disappointingly small).

Marathi
माझ्याकडे फक्त दहा रुपये आहेत (माझ्याकडे दहा रुपयांशिवाय काही नाही) — I have nothing but ten rupees. The negative ending matches the Marathi restrictive-negative construct.

Example
Easy: 財布に千円しかありません。(さいふに せんえんしか ありません。) — I have only 1,000 yen in my wallet. (Why: Restricts existence to a low count using a negative verb.)
Medium: ひらがなしか書けません。(ひらがなしか かけません。) — I can write nothing but hiragana. (Why: Shows limitation paired with a potential verb ending in the negative.)
Hard: 日曜日しか休むことができません。(にちようびしか やすむ ことが できません。) — I can only rest on Sundays. (Why: Couples the strict limitation particle with a negated capability phrase.)

Mnemonic
しか always needs its negative partner ない — think of them as a matched pair that can never separate.

AI Notes
Common mistake: forgetting the mandatory negative ending — しか literally cannot appear in an
affirmative sentence (❌ 千円しかあります); it must always end negative (千円しかありません), even
though the overall feel of the sentence isn't really "negative" in tone.

# Card G067: そして

Question
What does 「そして」mean?

Answer
And / And then (used at the beginning of a sentence to add more information or chronological steps).

Marathi
तो अभ्यास करतो आणि नंतर खेळतो — He studies and then plays. Both use sequential linkers, but '그리고' or 'आणि' starts clauses naturally.

Example
Easy: 京都へ行きました。そして、写真を撮りました。(きょうとへ いきました。そして、しゃしんを とりました。) — I went to Kyoto. And then, I took pictures. (Why: Links two sequentially completed historical actions.)
Medium: 彼女は親切です。そして、きれいです。(かのじょは しんせつです。そして、きれいです。) — She is kind. And also, she is beautiful. (Why: Connects two positive descriptive adjective sentences.)

Mnemonic
そして starts a brand-new sentence, bridging the previous idea to what comes next.

AI Notes
Common mistake: overusing そして to link every single sentence in a row — natural Japanese uses it
sparingly, often preferring て-form verb chaining or just context for closely related actions.

# Card G068: それから

What does 「それから」mean?

Answer
After that / Next / In addition (conjunction used to list subsequent actions or items).

Marathi
मी जेवलो, त्यानंतर झोपलो — I ate, after that I slept. Matches the Marathi chronological connector 'त्यानंतर' (tyananatar).

Example
Easy: 宿題をします。それから、テレビを見ます。(しゅくだいを します。それから、てれびを みます。) — I will do homework. After that, I will watch TV. (Why: Connects two planned future events in sequence.)

それ (that) + から (from) = "from that point" — i.e., "after that."

AI Notes
Common mistake: treating そして and それから as fully interchangeable — それから more strongly implies
a following STEP or additional item in a sequence, while そして is a more general, looser connective.

# Card G069: でも

Question
What does 「でも」mean?

Answer
But / However (used at the beginning of a sentence to introduce a contrast).

Marathi
तो हुशार आहे पण आळशी आहे — He is smart but lazy. The contrastive sentence opener corresponds directly to the Marathi 'पण' (pan) or 'परंतु' (parantu).

Example
Easy: 日本語は難しいです。でも、おもしろいです。(にほんごは むずかしいです。でも、おもしろいです。) — Japanese is difficult. But, it is interesting. (Why: Contrasts two opposing adjective sentences.)

Mnemonic
でも starts a new sentence with a "but wait..." tone, flipping the previous idea.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing sentence-starting でも (but/however) with the も-derived "even" meaning
attached to a noun (誰でも = "anyone," 何でも = "anything") — the position (sentence start vs.
attached to a word) tells the two uses apart.

# Card G071: から (so, conjunction)

Question
What does 「から (so, conjunction)」mean?

Answer
So / Therefore (placed mid-sentence after a reason clause to trigger a logical consequence).

Marathi
मला उशीर झाला म्हणून मी धावलो — I got late so I ran. The logical flow from reason (ending in から) to result matches the Marathi connector 'म्हणून' (mhanun).

Example

Medium: バスが来ませんでしたから遅れました。(ばすが きませんでしたから おくれました。) — The bus did not come, so I was late. (Why: Connects a past negative verbal event to a past consequence.)

Mnemonic
[Reason]から、[Result] — the reason always leads, から is the hinge, the result follows.

AI Notes
Common mistake: putting the result before the reason out of habit from English ("I was late because
...") — Japanese word order always states the reason clause FIRST, ending in から, before the result.

# Card G073: ~でしょうか

What does 「~でしょうか」mean?

I wonder if... / Could you tell me...? (a very polite, indirect question form).

मला ती मदत करेल का? — I wonder if she will help me?. The indirect, soft questioning tone aligns with using 'का?' softly on potential situations.

Example
Easy: これはだれの本でしょうか。(これは だれの ほんでしょうか。) — I wonder whose book this is. (Why: Softens a basic possessive question using でしょうか.)
Medium: 明日、雨が降るでしょうか。(あした、あめが ふるでしょうか。) — Do you think it will rain tomorrow? (Why: Expresses polite inquiry about a natural future event.)
Hard: この近くに郵便局があるでしょうか。(この ちかくに ゆうびんきょくが あるでしょうか。) — I wonder if there is a post office nearby? (Why: Asks softly about local existence of services.)

Mnemonic
でしょう (probably) + か (question) = "I wonder...?" — a softer, more polite way of asking indirectly.

AI Notes
Common mistake: using でしょうか as a blunt direct question — it's meant for SOFT, indirect wondering,
more polite than plain ですか, and often used when unsure or being extra courteous (e.g. asking a
stranger for directions).

Answer

# Card G075: ~つもりです

Question
What does 「~つもりです」mean?

Answer
Plan to / Intend to (used with dictionary form to state an active future intention or negative plan with ない-form).

Marathi
माझा जपानला जाण्याचा विचार आहे — I plan to go to Japan. The verbal noun structure matches 'विचार आहे' or 'योजना आहे' structurally.

Example
Easy: 来年日本へ行くつもりです。(らいねん にほんへ いくつもりです。) — I plan to go to Japan next year. (Why: Combines a future time marker with dictionary verb and つもりです.)

# Card G076: ~方 (かた)

Question
What does 「~方 (かた)」mean?

Answer
How to / Way of doing (attached to the verbます-stem; the resulting word becomes a noun).

Marathi
गाडी चालवण्याची पद्धत — The way of driving a car. The verbます-stem + 方 mirrors the Marathi construct 'क्रियापद + करण्याची पद्धत'.

Example
Easy: この漢字の読み方を教えてください。(この かんじの よみかたを おしえてください。) — Please teach me the reading (way of reading) of this kanji. (Why: Attaches 方 to the ます-stem of 読む to form a compound noun.)
Medium: お箸の使い方を知っていますか。(おはしの つかいかたを しっていますか。) — Do you know how to use chopsticks? (Why: Uses the ます-stem of 使う with the direct object marker の.)
Hard: 美味しい日本料理の作り方を習いました。(おいしい にほんりょうりの つくりかたを ならいました。) — I learned how to make delicious Japanese food. (Why: Connects adjective modifiers and compound noun creations under a past action verb.)

Mnemonic
方 (かた) on a verb's ます-stem turns "the act of doing X" into "the WAY of doing X" — 読み(ます)+方 =
読み方, "way of reading."

AI Notes
Common mistake: attaching 方 to the wrong verb form — it always attaches to the ます-stem, not the
dictionary form or て-form (読みます→読み方, not 読む方 or 読んで方). Also, this pattern is flagged in
the checklist itself as often being N4-level, so treat it as a bonus/preview pattern rather than a
core N5 requirement.
