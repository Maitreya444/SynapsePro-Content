# Card G050: ~ませんか
Question
What does 「~ませんか」mean?

Answer
Won't you...? / Would you like to...? (a polite invitation).

Marathi
तू माझ्यासोबत चहा पिणार नाहीस का? — Won't you drink tea with me?. Both languages politely invite someone using a negative verb question at the terminal sentence position.

Example
Easy: 一緒に京都へ行きませんか。 (いっしょにきょうとへいきませんか) — Won't you go to Kyoto with me? (Why: Formed by changing the ます form verb to ませんか to make a polite invitation.)
Medium: 今日、一緒に昼ご飯を食べませんか。 (きょう、いっしょにひるごはんをたべませんか) — Would you like to eat lunch together today? (Why: Time adverb and object are added before the invitation verb.)
Hard: 日曜日に図書館で一緒に勉強しませんか。 (にちようびにとしょかんでいっしょにべんきょうしませんか) — Won't you study together with me at the library on Sunday? (Why: Combines a specific day, place of action, and compound verb for a complex invitation.)

Mnemonic
ませんか = ません (won't) + か (question) — literally "won't you...?" — a gentle, indirect invite made
by asking the negative.

AI Notes
Common mistake: confusing ませんか (inviting: "won't you join me?") with ましょうか (offering: "shall I
do this for you?"). ませんか invites the listener to do something WITH you; ましょうか offers to do
something FOR them.

# Card G051: ~ましょう
Question
What does 「~ましょう」mean?

Answer
Let's (do something) (used to enthusiastically suggest or agree to a joint action).

Marathi
आपण अभ्यास करूया! — Let's study!. The Marathi suffix "-ऊया" (-uya) attached to the verb stem is the exact functional equivalent of the Japanese "~ましょう" to express collective intent.

Example
Easy: 休みましょう。 (やすみましょう) — Let's take a break. (Why: Volitional polite form 〜ましょう replaces 〜ます to propose a shared action.)
Medium: 駅で友達に会いましょう。 (えきでともだちにあいましょう) — Let's meet our friend at the station. (Why: Introduces place particle で and target particle に with the volitional verb.)
Hard: 宿題が終わりましたから、一緒に帰りましょう。 (しゅくだいがおわりましたから、いっしょにかえりましょう) — Since homework is finished, let's go home together. (Why: Includes a reason clause ending in から followed by the team suggestion.)

Mnemonic
ましょう sounds like "let's-show" — as in, "let's show up and do it together!"

AI Notes
Common mistake: using ましょう to state your own solo plan (that's たいです or つもりです instead) —
ましょう specifically proposes a SHARED action, implying "you too, let's."

# Card G052: ~ましょうか
Question
What does 「~ましょうか」mean?

Answer
Shall I / Shall we? (used to offer help or suggest an action to see if the other person agrees).

Marathi
मी तुला मदत करू का? — Shall I help you?. Adding the question particle "का" to the Marathi volitional verb form mirrors how "か" turns the joint suggestion "~ましょう" into a cooperative offer.

Example
Easy: 窓を開けましょうか。 (まどをあけましょうか) — Shall I open the window? (Why: Verb stem + ましょうか offers a helpful action to the listener.)
Medium: 重い荷物を持ちましょうか。 (おもいにもつをもちましょうか) — Shall I carry your heavy baggage? (Why: Tests the helper usage with a modified noun object.)
Hard: 暑いですから、エアコンをつけましょうか。 (あついですから、エアコンをつけましょうか) — Since it is hot, shall I turn on the air conditioner? (Why: Joins an adjective-based reason clause with a polite offer of assistance.)

Mnemonic
Same ましょう (let's) + か (question) = "shall we...?" — but in practice it's usually "shall I do this
for you?"

AI Notes
Common mistake: mixing up ましょうか (offering help) with ませんか (inviting company) — ましょうか
centers on the SPEAKER doing something for the listener, not both doing something together.

# Card G053: ~てください
Question
What does 「~てください」mean?

Answer
Please do (a polite request or mild instruction).

Marathi
कृपया येथे बसा. — Please sit here. Both languages place the polite request marker at the very end of the verb terminal position in their SOV syntax.

Example
Easy: ここに書いてください。 (ここにかいてください) — Please write here. (Why: Combines the verb's て-form with ください to formulate a polite instruction.)
Medium: この漢字を読んでください。 (このかんじをよんでください) — Please read this kanji. (Why: Applies a demonstrative adjective and direct object before the request verb.)
Hard: 辞書を使ってもいいですから、この紙に名前を書いてください。 (じしょをつかってもいいですから、このかみになまえをかいてください) — Since you may use a dictionary, please write your name on this paper. (Why: Chains a permission clause as a reason to a final polite instruction.)

Mnemonic
て-form + ください ("please give me") — literally asking someone to "give" you the action.

AI Notes
Common mistake: attaching ください straight onto the dictionary form (❌ 書くください) — it must follow
the て-form (書いてください). Also note てください is a fairly direct request; it's fine for
teachers/service contexts but can sound blunt to a superior in more formal settings.

# Card G054: ~ないでください
Question
What does 「~ないでください」mean?

Answer
Please don't (do something) (a polite request not to perform an action).

Marathi
कृपया काळजी करू नका. — Please do not worry. The Marathi prohibitive "नका" (naka) placed after the verb stem directly mirrors the terminal position of "~ないでください".

Example
Easy: 写真を撮らないでください。 (しゃしんをとらないでください) — Please don't take photos. (Why: Formed by taking the negative ない-form of the verb and attaching でください.)
Medium: 私を忘れないでください。 (わたしをわすれないでください) — Please do not forget me. (Why: Uses the direct object marker を with the negative polite request verb.)
Hard: ここは図書館ですから、大きな声で話さないでください。 (ここはとしょかんですから、おおきなこえではなさないでください) — Since this is a library, please do not speak in a loud voice. (Why: Integrates a copula reason clause with a negative request modified by an adverbial phrase.)

Mnemonic
ない (not) + でください (please) — "please, in the state of not doing this."

AI Notes
Common mistake: attaching ないでください to the ます-form (❌ 撮りますないでください) — it always follows
the plain ない-form of the verb (撮る → 撮らない → 撮らないでください).
